    <p><strong>Why Users Don't Trust AI Suggestions — And What To Do About It</strong></p>
    <p>New research from our AI UX group shows that users who can see <em>why</em> an AI made a recommendation are 2.4x more likely to act on it than users who see only the recommendation itself. Provenance matters more than accuracy.</p>
    <p style="text-align:center;margin:16px 0;">
      <a href="https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-trust/" style="background-color:#e8211a;color:#ffffff;padding:10px 20px;text-decoration:none;font-size:13px;font-weight:bold;">Read Article</a>

    <h3 style="font-family:Georgia,serif;font-size:16px;color:#1a1a1a;">Research Roundup</h3>
    <ul style="padding-left:20px;">
      <li><strong>Expert users skip onboarding</strong> — Highly expert users (lawyers, doctors, engineers) explore AI tools through task completion, not tutorials. Design for the first real task, not the first session.</li>
      <li><strong>Cognitive load and AI</strong> — When AI suggestions appear during high-stakes tasks, users experience a "decision interruption" that can increase stress and reduce overall throughput even when accuracy is high.</li>
      <li><strong>Inline vs. panel suggestions</strong> — Users preferred inline suggestions (within the content) over side-panel recommendations by a 3:1 margin across five tested domains.</li>
    </ul>
