<p style="font-family:Georgia,serif;font-size:18px;color:#1b3a5c;margin-top:0;"><strong>AI &amp; the Legal Profession</strong></p>
<p>This week's digest focuses on the intersection of generative AI and professional responsibility, a topic the ABA Standing Committee on Ethics and Professional Responsibility plans to address in formal guidance expected in Q3 2026.</p>

<hr style="border:none;border-top:1px solid #e5e7eb;margin:16px 0;">

<p style="font-family:Georgia,serif;font-size:16px;color:#1b3a5c;"><strong>ABA Formal Opinion 512: Six Months In</strong></p>
<p>Six months after the publication of Formal Opinion 512 on generative AI, bar associations in 22 states have issued their own guidance — nearly all of it consistent with the ABA's competence and supervision framework. The most common addition: state-level guidance on disclosure obligations when AI is used in client-facing work product. Three states now require disclosure; 11 have voluntary disclosure standards.</p>

<hr style="border:none;border-top:1px solid #e5e7eb;margin:16px 0;">

<p style="font-family:Georgia,serif;font-size:16px;color:#1b3a5c;"><strong>Contract Review: The Automation Bias Problem</strong></p>
<p>A new law review article from researchers at the University of Michigan examines how junior associates interact with AI-assisted contract review tools. Key finding: associates with less than three years of experience were significantly more likely to accept AI-flagged clause suggestions without independent verification, particularly under time pressure. The authors recommend supervision protocols and tool design changes that make the basis for AI suggestions visible to the reviewer.</p>

<hr style="border:none;border-top:1px solid #e5e7eb;margin:16px 0;">

<p style="font-family:Georgia,serif;font-size:16px;color:#1b3a5c;"><strong>In-House Outlook: Legal Ops Benchmarking Report</strong></p>
<p>The 2026 Corporate Legal Operations Consortium (CLOC) benchmarking report shows that 63% of in-house legal teams have piloted at least one AI tool in the past year, up from 34% in 2024. Adoption is highest for contract review (71%), research (58%), and first-draft generation (44%). The biggest reported barrier to full deployment: data security and residency concerns, cited by 68% of respondents.</p>
