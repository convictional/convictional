<p>Rhea,</p>

<p>Good to hear from you — I remember your final paper on standard-form enforcement, which was one of the sharper pieces I read that year.</p>

<p>The short answer to your question: the variation is mostly meaningful, not merely stylistic. The limitation of liability structure in SaaS follows the "consequential damages waiver + multiple-of-fees cap" pattern because the risk profile is information asymmetry (you can't see the bug coming). Professional services agreements tend to have lower caps relative to fees because there's more direct control over deliverables. Manufacturing supply agreements often have no cap at all in the event of product liability exposure — the whole structure breaks down when physical harm is foreseeable.</p>

<p>For published work: Schwarcz &amp; Scott's piece on relational contracts (Journal of Legal Studies, 2019) is the closest thing to what you're looking for. Also worth reading Kahan &amp; Klausner's work on standardization and network effects in contract drafting.</p>
