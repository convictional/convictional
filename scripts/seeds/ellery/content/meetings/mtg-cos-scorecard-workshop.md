  ## Summary
  Darren and Maren spent a full hour finishing the Chief of Staff scorecard so it could go to recruiter Leona at Halverson Partners. The meeting produced a complete scorecard — five "great at ninety days" bullets, three explicit non-goals, and two screener questions — and resolved the central framing debate in favor of an experienced operator rather than a strategist.

  ### Key Points
  - **Role definition settled:** Darren and Maren agreed the CoS role is about absorbing the coordination layer Darren currently carries himself — calendar triage, exec meeting prep, candidate debrief packages, the board update pipeline, and Keating deal coordination. The "second-in-command" framing was explicitly rejected.
  - **Ninety-day scorecard:** Five success criteria were drafted: Darren spends at least a third less time on coordination; every exec meeting has a pre-read and logged decisions; the board update is reviewed and edited, not written from scratch; Keating is progressing and Darren is never the internal bottleneck; the person is being pulled into conversations they weren't originally in.
  - **Three non-goals added:** Maren pushed to document what the role is not — running Keating (that's Tessa and Mateo), legal-product operations (Maren's lane), and proxy CEO. Darren flagged the third as the one he's most worried about signaling incorrectly to candidates.
  - **Screener questions agreed:** Two questions for Leona — "tell me about a time you had to say no to your CEO" and "walk me through a week where the priorities changed on Tuesday." Maren said the second will show whether a candidate operates from a system or is just a fast reactor.

  ### Challenges & Risks
  - **Candidate profile risk:** Darren is concerned the role might attract candidates who see it as a "CoS on the way to COO" path. The scorecard and screener questions are designed to filter for that, but the risk is real if the job description isn't written carefully.

  ### Agenda Review
  - **Missed Topics:** None — all three agenda items were completed.

[Darren]: Thanks for carving out the hour. I want to actually finish the scorecard today. I've been running with a half-written version in my head for two weeks and Leona needs something she can use.

[Maren]: Okay. What's the starting premise?

[Darren]: The premise is that this role is not a second-in-command. It's not a strategist. It's an operator whose job is to absorb the coordination layer I'm currently carrying — which is eating me.

[Maren]: That's clearer than you've been about this before. What does "the coordination layer" mean in concrete terms?

[Darren]: Calendar triage — which requests actually need me versus which can go to you or Tessa. Exec meeting prep — someone who makes sure every meeting I'm in has a clear agenda and a clear outcome. Candidate debrief packaging — right now I'm personally writing up every on-site, which is absurd. Board update pipeline — the monthly narrative, the financial appendix, the investor questions I don't have time to preempt. Keating deal coordination — not selling, but making sure I'm prepped and nobody's blocked.

[Maren]: That's five things. Is the scorecard five bullets?

[Darren]: I think so. Let me try it. At ninety days, great looks like: one — I spend at least a third less of my week on calendar and coordination work. Two — every exec meeting has a pre-read and clear decisions logged after. Three — the board update is something I review and edit, not something I write from scratch. Four — Keating is progressing and I'm never the bottleneck on internal questions. Five — the person has earned enough trust with the leadership team that they're being pulled into conversations they weren't originally in.

[Maren]: Good. I'd add a non-goal bullet, because the role could drift.

[Darren]: Three non-goals?

[Maren]: One — they are not running Keating. That's Tessa and Mateo. Two — they are not a legal-product operator, which is my lane. Three — they are not a proxy CEO. If a question needs you, it needs you.

[Darren]: Agreed. The third one is the one I'm most worried about signaling incorrectly. I don't want a candidate to show up thinking this is a "Chief of Staff to the CEO on their way to COO" role. It might become that, but not in year one.

[Maren]: Then the screener questions need to surface that. What were you thinking?

[Darren]: One — tell me about a time you had to say no to your CEO. I want to hear that they've done it, that it went well, and that they can articulate why they said no. Two — walk me through a week where the priorities changed on Tuesday.

[Maren]: I like the second one. It'll show me whether they operate from a system or whether they're just a fast reactor.

[Darren]: Okay. I'll send this to Leona tonight. Thank you for making me finish it.

[Maren]: Thank you for admitting you needed the help. That's half the reason you're hiring this person.

[Darren]: Don't be cute.

[Maren]: A little.
