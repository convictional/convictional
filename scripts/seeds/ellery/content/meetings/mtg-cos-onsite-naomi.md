[Darren — Panel 1]: Thanks for coming in. Before I get into the scorecard, I want to ask — why Ellery?

[Naomi]: I've watched the company from a distance since your seed round. A friend of mine does legal ops at a mid-market company and she's been raving about the redline tool for a year. When Leona reached out, I read the Series A announcement, looked at who's on the team, and thought — this is exactly the stage where the Chief of Staff role matters most.

[Darren]: Good. Let me give you the core question. If you started Monday and I said "spend your first week how you want," what does that week look like?

[Naomi]: Honestly? I'd spend Monday and Tuesday in listening mode. One-on-ones with you, Maren, Priya, Tessa, Leo. Not asking for deliverables — just trying to get a read on where the friction actually is. Wednesday I'd sit in on as many meetings as I'm allowed to. Thursday I'd draft a thirty-sixty-ninety plan and share it with you. Friday I'd come back with the one thing I want to own in the first thirty days and ask for your endorsement.

[Darren]: That's a good answer. What's the one thing, though? Just your best guess today.

[Naomi]: Based on what I've read and what Leona described, I'd guess the exec meeting system. You have a cadence but it sounds like the pre-reads and action-item tracking are ad hoc. That's a fast win and it gives me legitimacy with the team.

[Darren]: It's a fair instinct. Let me push on it a little — what if the one thing I need you to own is the Keating deal coordination, and the exec cadence is a nice-to-have? How does that change what you'd do?

[Naomi]: I'd ask you to be clear about what "coordination" means. If it's making sure every internal meeting has an owner and a decision tracked, I can do that on day one. If it's being in the deal conversations and pushing on pricing or security questions, that's not my lane and I'd tell you so.

[Darren]: Good. That's the right answer.

[Priya — Panel 2]: Hi Naomi, thanks for staying on. I want to ask about a specific scenario. Imagine it's two weeks from now. The roadmap is in open debate — Leo wants to swing big on an AI feature, Maren wants to close coverage gaps, Darren is wavering. How do you structure the week so a decision actually gets made?

[Naomi]: I'd start by asking Darren what decision format he wants — whether it's his call after input, or whether he's trying to get to consensus. Then I'd make sure each of Leo, Maren, and Priya has written down their position in a format the others can respond to. Probably a short memo each. I'd schedule the decision meeting with a hard deadline and send the memos forty-eight hours in advance. During the meeting, I'd keep the conversation on tradeoffs, not positions.

[Priya]: That's all facilitation. Where's the content judgment?

[Naomi]: The content judgment is yours and Leo's and Maren's. My job is to make sure the decision actually gets made on a reasonable timeline.

[Priya]: Fair. But there's a version of this role where the person isn't just a facilitator — they're someone who can read the memos and tell Darren "this is the framing that's missing." Do you want to do that part?

[Naomi]: I'd want to grow into it. I wouldn't pretend to have that instinct on day one for a legal AI product.

[Priya]: That's honest. Thank you.

[Tessa — Panel 3]: Last panel, I promise. Quick scenario — Mateo on my team calls you and says "Darren keeps getting pulled into my Keating emails and it's slowing the deal down." What do you do?

[Naomi]: I ask Mateo what specifically keeps pulling Darren in — the content of the emails, or the fact that customers are asking for the CEO. Then I go to Darren and propose a rule: for Keating, any customer email that comes to him gets triaged through me, and I'll pull him in only if it's CEO-level. I'd run that for two weeks and we'd measure.

[Tessa]: Good. One more — what do you do when Mateo pushes back and says he wants Darren in the loop because it helps the deal?

[Naomi]: I'd tell Mateo that "CEO in the loop" is not free, and I'd ask what specifically he wants from Darren — reassurance, signature, air cover, or actual content. If it's reassurance, I can write it. If it's content, Darren comes in.

[Tessa]: That's a good answer. Thank you for coming today.
