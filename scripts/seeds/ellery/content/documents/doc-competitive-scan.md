# Competitive Scan — AI-Native Redline Assistants

**Author:** Leo
**Updated:** This week, following Keating competitive debrief from Amira.
**Audience:** Exec team + GTM.

## The Landscape

Four real competitors in our segment, plus two adjacent categories that could collapse into us.

## Direct Competitors

### Harvey
- **Positioning:** general-purpose legal AI for BigLaw
- **Strengths:** brand recognition inside AmLaw 50, deeper penetration, strong founder story
- **Weaknesses:** wide but shallow. Playbook fidelity is generic, not firm-specific. Redline explainability is weak.
- **How we frame against them:** "Harvey assists. Ellery does the first-pass." Lean on playbook depth and the ground-truth audit.

### Spellbook
- **Positioning:** Word-plugin-first redline and drafting for small and mid-market firms
- **Strengths:** easy onboarding, low ACV, good Word integration
- **Weaknesses:** not designed for playbook-heavy BigLaw workflows. Coverage is shallow.
- **How we frame against them:** we are not in this segment's lane and vice versa. Positioning divergence, not competitive collision.

### Luminance
- **Positioning:** incumbent in contract analysis, evolving into AI redline
- **Strengths:** established enterprise relationships, trusted name
- **Weaknesses:** older ML stack. Redline quality is visibly a generation behind. Innovation committees have moved on from them at three firms we've talked to.
- **How we frame against them:** acknowledge their incumbency; show our quality gap transparently.

### Ironclad CLM AI
- **Positioning:** AI features added to an existing CLM platform
- **Strengths:** existing customer base, integrated with the CLM workflow
- **Weaknesses:** CLM-native, in-house-counsel-native. Doesn't fit the law-firm buying motion.
- **How we frame against them:** "We are built for firms. They are built for in-house counsel." Cleanly adjacent.

## Adjacent Categories That Could Collapse Into Us

### General-purpose LLM copilots (ChatGPT Enterprise, Claude for Legal)
Firms are buying "AI seats" for associates. These are a real threat — not because they are good at playbook-aware redlines (they aren't), but because they occupy the budget line a firm has for "AI for the practice." Our defense: we beat them on playbook fidelity and we integrate where their workflows actually live.

### Practice-specific tools
There are early-stage companies building practice-specific AI (M&A due diligence, real estate leasing). They could expand into commercial contracts. We should track them quarterly, not monthly.

## The "How Do They Talk About The Problem" Matrix

Worth naming because it shows where we can stand alone:

- **Harvey:** "AI-powered legal research and drafting"
- **Spellbook:** "AI contract drafting in Word"
- **Luminance:** "AI contract intelligence"
- **Ironclad:** "AI for your contract lifecycle"
- **Ellery:** "Playbook-aware first-pass redlines. With citations."

We are the only one naming the playbook, the only one naming citations, and the only one naming "first-pass" (which is what lawyers actually do). That is our language moat. Hold it.

## Recommendations

1. Sharpen the homepage one-liner around playbook + citations. Do not dilute.
2. Commission a side-by-side demo video (Ellery vs. Harvey) on a representative MSA. We have the quality to win a head-to-head today.
3. Revisit quarterly. This space is moving fast; competitive positioning at +90 days is not the same as today.
