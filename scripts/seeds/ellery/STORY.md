# Ellery Seed Story

## 1. Overview

Ellery is an 11-person, AI-native contract intelligence startup that sells a redline co-pilot and light CLM to in-house legal teams. They closed a $16M Series A led by Thornburgh Ventures three weeks before the snapshot, and the team has been sprinting ever since. The company was founded by Darren Okafor, an ex-infrastructure engineer and second-time founder, and Maren Kovacs, a former General Counsel who spent a decade inside an AmLaw firm before deciding the real leverage was in the software lawyers touch every day. Ellery has about eighteen paying customers, mostly mid-market in-house teams, and a pipeline that now includes their first true AmLaw 100 prospect: Keating & Marsh.

The emotional texture is *excited and overwhelmed*. The bank account looks different than it did a month ago, the board expects a step-change in execution, and the team — bonded by scarcity for two years — is suddenly staring at the pressure of abundance. Everyone wants to hire first. Everyone has a plan for the Series A announcement. The founders are running on adrenaline and a shared sense that they are two weeks away from something going wrong in a way they can't yet predict. Darren, in particular, is drowning in coordination: he is the scheduler, the recruiter, the enterprise seller of last resort, the roadmap tiebreaker, and the board whisperer, all at once. Maren sees it. The team sees it. Nobody has said the phrase "Darren needs a Chief of Staff" out loud as often as the current reality requires, but the hire is on the table and candidates are in loop. The story captures the four weeks when Ellery is genuinely trying to become a grown-up company without losing the intimacy that got them here.

## 4. The 5 Narrative Threads

### Thread 1 — Hiring Plan (the driver)
The Series A came with a 22-over-12-months hiring envelope. Every function wants to go first. Priya wants SRE + second ML + integrations engineer. Leo wants a senior designer and a PM. Tessa wants two more AEs and a first SDR. Maren wants a second legal content analyst and whispers "a lightweight content-ops hire." Darren wants a CoS yesterday.

The *Hire* goal is the parent goal. Most of the specific function hires roll up here, but the CoS has its own top-level goal because of its strategic weight (and because Darren, for once, is the single accountable party).

**Emotional texture:** Post-raise optimism collides with the fact that there isn't enough bandwidth to hire seven roles at once. The story shows Priya's hiring plan landing, some gentle turf defense in comments, and the quiet emergence of a *sequencing* (not slimming) consensus.

**Key artifacts:** Priya's engineering hiring plan doc; the job req spreadsheet in a document; recruiter threads in email; a *Hiring* weekly recurring meeting; a Decision post ("What order do we hire the first four roles?") that will be closed during the window.

### Thread 2 — Chief of Staff hire
Darren is drowning. Maren sees it. The scorecard was drafted over a weekend, the shortlist is real, and the interview loops have happened. Three candidates. Three interview debrief docs. Two references called. One final-round today.

**Emotional texture:** This is the emotional through-line. The team can see that Darren is at the edge. Nobody is being precious about it. The loop is happening, and the decision will happen during the window. The texture in private (founders DM) is more vulnerable than the texture in public (Exec chat, interview debriefs).

**Key artifacts:** CoS scorecard document; interview guide; three candidate debrief documents; Darren↔Maren DM; recruiter emails; references-called doc; interview-loop meetings.

### Thread 3 — Keating & Marsh (the marquee enterprise deal)
AmLaw 100 firm. Would be Ellery's largest customer by 3x if landed. Requires SOC 2 (at minimum Type I now, Type II on commitment) and EU data residency (they have offices in London and Frankfurt). They want paper-grade security diligence, a scaled pilot, and — quietly — a CISO-friendly champion at Ellery, which they interpret as a sign that Ellery is ready for them.

**External cameos:**
- Amira Saleh, General Counsel's Office, Keating & Marsh (amira.saleh@keatingmarsh.com) — senior counsel running legal ops for the buying committee, primary champion.
- Doug Rennert, CISO, Keating & Marsh (doug.rennert@keatingmarsh.com) — security lead, owns the questionnaire.
- Peter Nakagawa, Partner at Keating & Marsh (peter.nakagawa@keatingmarsh.com) — economic buyer, the partner sponsoring the evaluation.
- Brandt Soh, Senior Auditor, Halden & Rossi (brandt.soh@haldenrossi.com) — SOC 2 auditor on Ellery's side.

**Emotional texture:** Equal parts thrilled and scared. The deal validates the enterprise motion. Losing it would not kill the company but it would kill a Q3 narrative. Internally there is real tension about whether it makes sense to say yes to EU residency *right now*. Tessa and Mateo are pulling for go; Arjun and Priya are cautious about scope.

**Key artifacts:** Deal strategy doc; SOC 2 readiness doc; EU residency ADR; Keating demo script; the proposal (draft); Keating security questionnaire work-back; three deal-review meetings plus one recurring Keating prep; the GTM chat and Tessa↔Mateo DM.

### Thread 4 — Post-Raise Roadmap (Leo vs. Maren)
Leo's *Redline Co-pilot* PRD proposes the most ambitious feature in Ellery's history: an AI assistant that sits inside contract review and proactively suggests edits based on the customer's playbook plus the user's prior redlines. Maren's *Coverage-First* memo argues the team should close basic contract-type coverage (DPAs, MSAs, specific industry playbooks) before stacking a speculative feature on top of it. Darren is genuinely torn.

The debate is the first *real* strategic disagreement at the exec level that the team will resolve in full view of the company — which is itself a maturation signal. Emma's research is the tiebreaker data. Jordan has a working prototype behind a flag. Coverage gaps are measurable and Rhea has the data.

**Emotional texture:** Intellectual, not personal. Leo and Maren genuinely like each other. But the outcome matters enormously, and the public form of the debate — Exec chat, a decision post, two comment threads on the PRD and the memo — is the thread that the rest of the company is learning *how to have disagreement* by watching.

**Key artifacts:** Leo's PRD doc; Maren's coverage memo; Emma's research synthesis doc; Jordan's eval report doc; a competitive scan doc; the *Ship* decision post; engineering chat surface; Legal Product chat surface.

### Thread 5 — Operating Rhythm Reset
First real board meeting is six weeks out. Ellery has never had company-level OKRs. Lyndon Brace (new board member) has gently asked to see an operating rhythm document on his first board-prep call. Maren and Priya have both said that the lack of structure is starting to hurt. Darren has been promising an all-hands for three weeks and hasn't given one. An exec offsite is being loosely planned.

**Emotional texture:** This is the "we are becoming a grown-up company" thread. Some nostalgia (scrappy-is-fun), some excitement (finally!), some low-grade awkwardness (we should have done this months ago). Tessa and Priya are the pragmatists here; Maren quietly helps Darren put the narrative together.

**Key artifacts:** Company OKRs draft doc; exec operating rhythm proposal doc; board deck draft doc; all-hands narrative doc; values / how-we-decide doc; two meetings plus one recurring exec sync; board-and-investor update emails; All-Hands chat.
